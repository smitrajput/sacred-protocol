# Risk

How your funds are protected, what was tested, and what each result shows.

## How your funds are protected

Your funds are protected by 4 layers of backstops before a loss touches your principal.

<LossOrder />

Primary and tail protection are cash pots Sacred fills from its 23% share of the markup, after essential operating costs. At the 2× tier they are sized at 0.4% and 0.6% of approved financing, which is the total the vault has committed to finance, used or not, since unused capacity can be drawn at any time. For example, $9.5m approved means $38,000 primary and $57,000 tail. In the model they take about 15 months to fill at $10m and 90% use.

**The pilot backstop covers the start.** A small part of the money Sacred raises from investors protects Earn and Borrow during the pilot, while primary and tail protection fill up.

Earn funds Trade and Borrow through two separate vault contracts. A loss in the Trade vault can't touch the Borrow vault or the company's operating cash, and the reverse holds too. The Borrow vault has its own 4 layers: the borrower's collateral, the primary and tail reserve sleeves, and the pilot backstop during its pilot.

On top of that order, 4 controls apply:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Wallet control** | You hold your own keys, through an embedded wallet you can export or a wallet you connect |
| **Contract security** | New contracts go through independent review before launch, followed by a small, capped pilot |
| **Collateral controls** | Pledged assets are never lent, staked or reused. Coverage limits, price checks and depth caps control every sale |
| **Withdrawal conditions** | Withdrawals rank ahead of new financing. Earn pays from idle cash, then a daily queue. Repayment and exit stay open during pauses |

The layers cover trading losses. They do not cover a failure of a contract, an oracle, USDC or the chain. Those are listed under [known risks](#known-risks-across-all-three-products).

## Reading the numbers

Three kinds of number appear below and they are different things:

| Kind | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Historical loss** | What a replay of past prices produced | No principal loss at 2× in the Binance normal replay |
| **Planning allowance** | A budget line for expected loss | 5 bps a year on financing |
| **Policy cushion** | Cash chosen to absorb losses | 1% of approved financing |

None of them is a guarantee. All results use price history from centralized exchanges, and nothing here was run on Arc.

### The full hourly history

Binance BTC and ETH, January 2018 to September 2026, about 76,000 overlapping 14-day cohorts per asset and configuration. Missing timestamps are kept as outages, so bad windows stay in.

| Scenario, 2× | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Normal, 1 hour | No principal loss |
| Four-hour delay, 3% friction, Binance | No principal loss |
| Four-hour delay, 3% friction, OKX ETH | **Six losing daily cohorts, worst individual loss 3.42%.** Maximum annual book loss 0.835% of nominal ETH capacity, about 0.418% on an equal BTC and ETH book |
| Bybit, July 2021 onward | No normal-case principal loss |
| 24-hour outage, 5% friction, Binance | **Worst individual loss 21.71% BTC, 27.99% ETH.** Worst rolling year 14.45% of peak financing |

The 24-hour case is a separate emergency. The 1% routine stock does not cover it.

Partial sales are ordinary. In replay, 9.4%, 11.2% and 14.3% of 2× positions entered in bull, sideways and bear conditions saw a forced sale.

Normal annual principal-loss rates are zero at 2× and 2.5×, and about 0.107%, 0.68% and 1.18% of actual financing at 3×, 4× and 5×.

### The realistic-delay panel

224 re-verified minute archives: 105 systematic monthly dates plus seven stress dates, for each asset. Each observation starts at the trigger and takes the worst fall over the detection minute plus five more, with 1% exit friction. It shows how bad a fast drop could be, not how often sales happen.

| Maximum shortfall on stress dates | BTC | ETH |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 3× at 130% | 0% | 0% |
| 4× at 120% | 1.07% | 4.80% |
| 5× at 115% | 5.19% | 8.77% |

The largest stress-date gaps were 16.71% on BTC and 19.85% on ETH. Every tier had zero shortfall on the systematic dates. The windows overlap, so these results can't be read as yearly odds.

### A saturated mix

At the most concentrated mix the caps allow, counting correlated losses together, the worst rolling annual loss is 0.186% in the one-hour case and 0.856% in the four-hour case, against total approved capacity. ES99 is 0.336% and 1.489%. The 2.6% stock covers those modeled cases. The 24-hour emergency still loses 16.53% in the worst historical rolling year.

Month resampling produced 30,000 one-year paths from 103 complete months, so the paths reuse the same history.

### What SIFT adds, and what it does not

Account-level limits are added on top of the position-level ones, and they measure different things.

| | Coverage | Account drawdown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Measures** | One position's asset against its debt | One account's unit NAV against its high-water mark |
| **Trigger** | 160% at 2× | 10% pauses growth, 20% halves capacity, 30% freezes it |
| **Effect** | Partial sale of the asset | Less or no new financing. Existing sales unchanged |

- **Common shocks.** Many wallets buying BTC can all be liquidated together. Aggregate asset and route limits are still needed, whatever the score says. Production limits must come from real execution data. The 1%-of-financing protection is not enough for every possible crash.
- **Sticky limits.** Risk ceilings and freezes have no automatic recovery in v1.
- **Identity.** Wallet splitting is limited by no double pledge, one newcomer budget and aggregate caps. There is no identity check.
- **Brought records.** A record from another venue can start an account at up to $50,000. It can be bought, rented or picked from several accounts, and exchange records rest on an attester. The trader's own capital beneath the financing, a 10% budget for unconfirmed records and the first repayment checkpoint limit the damage. None of this is tested.
- **Oracles.** Canonical daily prices and rounds are versioned settings still to be chosen. Stale or missing data stops new financing.
- **Selection can backfire.** Aggressive selection can leave capital idle and reduce income.

## Borrow

Borrow's policy is set per collateral, with liquidation and restoration measured against full contractual debt:

| Collateral | Entry | Liquidation | Restoration |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| cirBTC | **55%** | **70%** | **60%** |
| WETH | **50%** | **65%** | **55%** |

These levels are judgment calls. They replaced a single 45% / 62.5% / 50% policy on 24 November 2026, to give borrowers more USDC per dollar of collateral. The tests below were rerun with the same model for the new levels, with every sale opened at its limit. The rerun has not been independently audited, and the Bybit and minute-gap checks were not repeated.

| Test, 2018 to 2026, 30-day sales at 8% | BTC annual loss on financing | ETH annual loss on financing | Equal book |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Binance one hour, 0.1% friction | 0 | 0 | No shortfall |
| Binance four-hour path, 3% | 0 | 0.0002% | Worst year **0.0009%**, ES99 **0.0014%** |
| OKX four-hour path, 3% | 0 | 0.0488% | Worst year **0.195%**, ES99 **0.313%** |
| Binance 24-hour path, 5% | 2.1838% | 2.2398% | Worst year **17.14%**, ES99 **26.34%** |

Sources are Binance and OKX hourly history. Kraken lacks comparable history and no replay is claimed for it. At the cirBTC trigger of 70%, gross collateral is 1.43 times full debt, leaving 30% of further price fall before debt exceeds collateral, less slippage and costs. At the WETH trigger of 65% the figures are 1.54 times and 35%.

**What the higher limits cost.** Against the earlier 45% policy, the four-hour OKX ES99 about doubles, from 0.162% to 0.313%, and the day-long ES99 rises from 18.55% to 26.34%. Borrowers at the limit are liquidated about twice as often on BTC. A 60% entry limit is excluded: in the four-hour case it either lost principal or left under 14% of price room before liquidation.

**What the 1% reserve covers.** It is about 3.2 times the four-hour ES99, down from 6.2 times under the earlier policy. The margin is chosen by judgment. The day-long emergency would need about 26% of financing, so the reserve does not cover it. Investors bear that loss.

**Sale frequency.** In healthy-repayment replay, 17.85% of BTC and 25.67% of ETH daily cohorts saw a price liquidation, up from 8.99% and 19.31% under the earlier policy.

**Route controls.** A route's fixed-debt book cannot exceed 25% of independently measured USDC bid depth within five minutes at 1% slippage. Common bridge, oracle or custodian exposure counts together.

**Maturity concentration.** At most 5% of approved financing due in one day, and 30% in seven.

**Price controls.** New-risk pricing must be 60 seconds fresh or better, with two independently operated cross-checks. Over 1% disagreement, or USDC outside 0.98 to 1.02, freezes new risk. Debt health prices USDC at `max(1, validated USDC/USD)`, so a depeg cannot create borrowing power. Repayment and top-ups stay open. Enforcement needs valid prices and quotes within limits.

**Governance.** Risk increases have a 35-day timelock, apply prospectively and stay within the mandate. Reductions and pauses can be immediate.

## Known risks across all three products

| Risk | What could happen | What limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract bug | Loss of deposits or pledged assets | Audits, caps and a capped pilot. No audit has happened |
| Stale or wrong price | Bad sales, or new risk at a wrong price | Freshness and cross-checks. Enforcement needs valid prices |
| Thin Arc markets | Sales fail or fill badly | Depth caps and route limits. Arc depth is unmeasured, and a route is at zero capacity until proven |
| Keeper delay | Sales arrive late | A 60-second target, unmeasured. Keepers are permissionless |
| Wrapper or bridge failure | cirBTC or WETH loses value or redemption | Independent wrapper checks and route caps. BTC and ETH prices cannot prove backing |
| USDC freeze or depeg | Circle can block an address. A depeg hits every pool | New-risk bands. Nothing inside the design fixes a freeze |
| Arc outage | No sales or exits during a halt | Nothing inside the design |
| Withdrawal wait | Earn depends on settlement. Borrow investors are committed for 12 months | Withdrawal priority, idle floors and maturity limits |
| Admin keys | A captured key changes future settings | Timelocks, hard limits, and pauses that protect exits |
| Regulation | Eligibility differs by country. Leverage is restricted in some, including Türkiye | Legal review before each market |
| Shariah | A board declines a key structure | The design changes. See [Shariah](/docs/shariah) |

## Unknown or untested

- Live depth, spreads, MEV, keeper and oracle behavior on Arc.
- Real borrower repayment and real trader behavior.
- Depositor willingness to accept conservation terms.
- Anything a professional audit would find.
- A crash worse than the history above, which can happen.
