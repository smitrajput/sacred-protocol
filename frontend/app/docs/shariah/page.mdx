# Shariah

The design rests on three contracts that all four Sunni schools accept in principle. Ten points still need a board's ruling, and none has been given. This page is the founder's structural analysis with sources, written so a scholar can check each claim against the code, and with the design's own rough odds for each ruling.

## The three contracts

| Contract | Where | Rules built into the design | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mudarabah** | The vault | Profit split by ratio; loss falls on capital; no guarantee; the manager may co-invest | Usmani pp. 36, 47–49 |
| **Murabaha** | The sale in each ticket | The seller owns the coin first; cost is disclosed; the price is fixed at the sale and may exceed the cash price; it never rises on default; no roll-over; no stipulated rebate | Usmani pp. 70–71, 91, 98–100; OIC Fiqh Academy Res. 51 and 64 |
| **Rahn** | The pledge in each ticket | The sold item may be pledged back to the seller; the pledge stays the buyer's property; any surplus on sale returns to the buyer | Usmani pp. 88–89; Res. 51; Musa, HBKU, 2021 |

The design is not a down-payment sale (*'urbun*), because nothing is forfeited. It is not an option, because nothing is sold apart from the coin (Res. 63 bars options as a traded right).

## The 2006 ruling, answered

The Muslim World League's Fiqh Council ruled against margin trading for four reasons.

| The ruling's reason | This design |
| --- | --- |
| Fees charged for delay are riba | The price is fixed once and no charge ever grows. After the due date the coin is simply sold |
| A loan tied to a sale | There is no loan; the financing is itself a sale |
| Non-compliant instruments and selling what is not owned | Real coins, bought and owned by the vault before it sells them, within the same transaction |
| Excessive borrowing and risk-taking | Low leverage ceilings and a loss capped at the pledge |

The fourth reason is not fully answered by structure. It is why the leverage ceiling belongs to the board.

## What the design does on purpose

- **Possession before sale.** The vault buys and holds the coin before recording the sale, inside one transaction. Whether that counts as possession (*qabd*) is ruling 4.
- **No sale back to the vault.** The pledged coin is sold only on the open market. A seller buying back what it sold on credit is a prohibited buy-back. There is no organised tawarruq.
- **Limited recourse, drafted carefully.** The price stays fixed; the contract limits the *means of collection* to the pledged coin. It is never priced separately: one fixed price, no protection line, no cheaper version without it.
- **No penalties and no roll-over.** Nothing can add to a balance. A trader who wants to stay in opens a new ticket, a fresh purchase through the market. Extending a debt for an extra charge is riba (Usmani pp. 98–99).
- **The early-settlement waiver is policy, not a term.** A rebate for early payment is allowed only if it is not stipulated in the contract (Usmani pp. 99–100; Res. 64).
- **Stakers as loss-bearing partners.** Those who stand behind shortfalls are paid from the traders' profit share, in USDC, and lose their stake first. A backstop paid for a guarantee would be a paid guarantee, which is barred; a donated, mutual or true loss-sharing backstop is not.
- **Wording.** The product says "no liquidation price; price drops do not close your position before your due date", not "liquidation-free". It says "you own the coin; it is held in your ticket", not "protected". Advertising a policy turns it into a term.

## Rulings to clear

Rough judgements from the design work, not a scholar's view, assuming a board aligned with AAOIFI that already accepts crypto as property.

| # | Ruling | Needed for | Estimate | Basis and fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ETH can be sold on deferred payment as a commodity | Both modes | 85–90% | Malaysia's Securities Commission; Amanie's ETH paper |
| 2 | BTC can be sold on deferred payment | Both modes | 65–75% | Scholars who treat BTC as money would bar this |
| 3 | Wrapped BTC counts as possessing BTC | Both modes | 60–70% | No published position found |
| 4 | Owning the coin within one transaction counts as possession | Both modes | 65–75% | Fallback: two transactions, the vault holding the coin between |
| 5 | Vault terms: weekly exit at share price, a loss reserve, non-transferable shares | Both modes | 85–90% | Standard mudarabah practice |
| 6 | Early-settlement waiver as a published policy | Both modes | 60–70% | A fixed on-chain formula may look stipulated. Fallback: shorter terms |
| 7 | Shortfall at the due date waived as policy | Term, if 8 fails | about 75% | Forgiving an insolvent debtor is encouraged |
| 8 | Limited recourse written into the contract | Term; a launch requirement | 60–70% | No precedent found. Fallback: a mutual shortfall fund (takaful, Res. 238), which may need an insurance licence |
| 9 | Selling the pledge before the due date when its value falls | Margin mode | 65–75% | Existing Islamic margin products do this at 2x |
| 10 | Leverage ceiling | Both modes | 2x about 85%; 3x 60–70%; 5x 35–45%; 10x 15–25% | No precedent above 2x |

The rulings are not independent, so the odds should not be multiplied. Rough estimates for whole packages: term mode at 2x, 50 to 60%; term mode at 3x, 40 to 50%; margin mode at 10x, 10 to 20%. If the board says no to any of them, the design changes; most have a known fallback.

SCR adds rulings with no estimate given: the trader's undertaking to donate a share of profit to a mutual fund; the stakers' reward; a mudarabah in which the manager takes no share of the markup; SCR itself and the reserve sale as a spot sale delivered staked; the pool fee.

## Precedent and limits

- Precedent exists at 2x: Mawarid Securities in the UAE runs murabaha margin at 2x with forced sale at a 30% maintenance level (Musa, 2021). Kenanga's Islamic margin charges 10.75 to 11% a year.
- No fatwa covers leverage on crypto, approving or rejecting.
- Some scholars reject crypto itself. A June 2026 fatwa from Darul Uloom Karachi holds that crypto tokens are not property. A board following it would reject the whole design.
- The board is chosen with rulings 4, 6, 8 and 10 in hand: candidates are asked their position on these before engagement.

## Not in this design

| Left out | Why | What would bring it back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shorts | The only classical contract for selling what one does not hold is a prepaid forward (*salam*), which needs its own market and rulings | A later phase |
| Rolling a ticket over | Extending a debt for an extra charge is riba | Never |
| Gold tokens | Gold must be exchanged on the spot | Never |
| Synthetic perps on promises | Nothing is owned; cash settlement is barred (Res. 40–41, 157, 238) | Not expected |
| Hedging the bucket's risk | Every available hedge is a short or a derivative | Nothing currently |
| More coins | Each needs a deep spot market and its own ruling | SOL and BNB next |
