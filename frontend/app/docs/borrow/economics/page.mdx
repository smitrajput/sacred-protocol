# Pricing and reserves

New Borrow offers are priced against the market, aiming at the lowest sustainable borrower price and the highest investor profit share the product can support. **8% with a 75% investor share is the audited reference.** It is not a launch quote. Both launch values are unset until an offer is approved. Sacred has a measurable selection process, not evidence of an optimal or cheapest price.

These are research scenarios as of 2 November 2026. All of them exclude Trade income, token income and any yield on idle cash.

## How profit is divided

Use `F` for actual time-weighted financing, `A` for approved capacity including commitments, `r` for the new-sale quote and `p` for the agreed investor share.

```text
planning net investment result = F × (r − 0.05% annual loss allowance)
distributable profit = positive surplus after direct costs and all loss restoration
manager              = (1 − p) × distributable profit
LP entitlement       = p × distributable profit
IRR retention        = min(20% × LP entitlement, max(0, 1% × A − eligible IRR))
company operating profit = manager revenue − company operating costs
```

IRR is the **investment risk reserve**. It belongs to investors, and it is funded from their profit entitlement. It is not company money and it is not a guarantee.

| Of each 100 of distributable profit | 75% reference | 80% candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investor cash | 60 | 64 |
| Investor-owned IRR | 15 | 16 |
| Manager, held in escrow | 25 | 20 |

Once the reserve reaches target, investors receive their full agreed share in cash, and retention resumes only if it is needed. Manager allocations stay in escrow until final vintage true-up, and any overallocation returns to investors. The 5 bps allowance is a budget assumption, not a second cash reserve.

## Protection

The target is **1% of approved financing**, including commitments: 0.4% primary and 0.6% tail. Those are two sleeves of one investor-owned stock, and each contribution splits 40/60. Unused approved capacity still needs protection.

The reserve starts empty in the pilot, when the pilot backstop, a small portion of the capital Sacred raises from investors, stands behind it. Growth beyond the pilot needs the full target, and depletion pauses new risk until the stock and any loss are restored. The 1% is a judgment, about 6.2 times the largest modeled operating-stress ES99. It does not cover a day-long outage, as the [risk page](/docs/risk#borrow) shows.

Costs exclude initial development, legal and audits. The company needs $125,000 of first-year funding, because manager allocations sit in escrow, and $187,500 keeps the six-month floor intact.

## The price tradeoff

Raising the investor share moves company revenue. It creates no new income.

| $10m, 85% use, steady budget | Investor cash APR | Company net | First-vintage company net |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8% / 75%, reference | 4.05% | $43,938 | $31,027 |
| 7.5% / 80%, conditional candidate | 4.05% | $1,650 | −$7,979 |
| 6% / 80%, lower-price sensitivity | 3.24% | −$23,850 | −$31,384 |

At 70% use, 7.5%/80% loses $20,700 a year. Lower prices need shown demand, more scale or verified lower costs. The alternative vintage figures are projections, not new liquidation or reserve replays.

**New-offer rule.** Compare all-in costs over the same tenor, validate demand, and require conservative company cost coverage for mature offers. A capped pilot deficit must be fully budgeted from company cash with the runway floor intact, never from investor principal or the reserve. Investor share is fixed at vintage subscription and each sale price at acceptance. New quotes never amend old debts or investor rights.

## How competitors compare

To compare fairly, normalize an APR as `F × r × 30 / 365` and an APY as `F × ((1 + APY)^(30/365) − 1)`, then add applicable fees.
