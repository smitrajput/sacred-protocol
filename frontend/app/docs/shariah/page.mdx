# Shariah

Sacred is built from classical contracts, and no board has reviewed it. This page is the founder's structural analysis with sources, written so that a scholar can check each claim, and it states exactly what still needs approval. Until then nothing here should be read as a ruling, and Sacred makes no certified claim.

## Principles

- **Own before you sell.** The seller owns the asset, with real risk, before the sale.
- **A known price.** The sale price and profit are fixed at the sale, in dollars or USDC units.
- **No increase for delay.** No late charge, no compounding and no rollover.
- **Security stays the owner's.** Pledged assets belong to the buyer, and surplus returns to the buyer.
- **Capital shares real outcomes.** Investors share actual profit by ratio and bear capital losses, and no return is guaranteed.

## Why Trade takes no share of profit

Mandatory trader profit share is 0% at every tier. AAOIFI's Murabaha standard, clauses 4/6 to 4/7, requires the sale price and profit to be known at signing and rejects profit set by future variations. [SBP](https://archive.sbp.org.pk/press/Essentials/Essentials%20of%20Islamic.htm) also requires a certain price. A charge that depends on later trading profit is not established as part of a fixed sale. A separate partnership would change how losses fall and needs its own structure, and no income from one is assumed.

## The 2006 ruling, answered

The Muslim World League's Fiqh Council ruled against margin trading for four reasons.

| The ruling's reason | This design |
| --- | --- |
| Fees charged for delay are riba | The price is fixed once. No charge grows, and there is no late markup |
| A loan tied to a sale | There is no loan. The financing is itself a sale |
| Non-compliant instruments and selling what is not owned | Real assets, bought and owned by the vault before it sells them |
| Excessive borrowing and risk-taking | Leverage capped at 5×, with higher tiers opened gradually as rulings are approved |

The fourth reason is not fully answered by structure, which is why the leverage ceiling belongs to a board. Precedent exists at 2×: Mawarid Securities in the UAE runs murabaha margin at 2× with forced sale at a 30% maintenance level (Musa, 2021), and Kenanga's Islamic margin charges 10.75 to 11% a year.

## Arc and USDC

Borrow rests on a premise. Sacred **accepts as a working assumption that USDC on Arc qualifies as a commodity**. On Arc, USDC is the native gas token: the thing you spend to use the network. That gives it a use beyond being money, which is the core of the argument, and it is why Arc gives Sacred stronger ground for Shariah approvals than a chain where USDC is only a payment token.

If that holds, selling USDC for a fixed deferred USDC price can be a sale of a commodity rather than a loan of money. That premise and the sale are different questions:

| Question | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Is USDC on Arc a commodity? | Assumed, not ruled on. Scholars may reject it |
| Is this owned-inventory sale, payable in USDC, a non-ribawi sale? | Not approved. Separate from the commodity question |
| Is the binding rebate permissible, including partial and forced settlement? | Not approved |
| Are the mudarabah pool and reserve deed acceptable? | Not approved |

Commodity status does not settle the rest. AAOIFI's qard standard covers fungible wealth generally and prohibits stipulated excess, and its Murabaha standard excludes deferred currency, gold and silver transactions. None of the reviewed authorities approves this particular USDC exchange. Arc gas utility alone does not select an approved cash mechanism.

A no-profit qard is a possible fallback. It delivers USDC and recovers the same amount plus independently permissible service costs. It would not fund any of Borrow's modeled investor returns, so it would be a different product.

## What needs review

Nothing below has been ruled on.

### Trade and Earn

1. Whether BTC and ETH can be sold on deferred payment as commodities.
2. Whether cirBTC and bridged WETH count as possessing the underlying asset.
3. Whether ownership within one transaction satisfies genuine ownership and possession (*qabd*). The fallback is two transactions with the vault holding between.
4. The known fixed price and profit, as implemented.
5. The pledge, and selling part of it before maturity when coverage falls.
6. Any early-settlement rebate. Binding and discretionary forms differ.
7. Limited recourse: collection stops at the pledged asset, so a trader never owes more than the pledge.
8. The leverage ceiling.
9. The depositor mandate, vintages and conservation offers.
10. Manager-funded protection and the pilot backstop. The Borrow research found that AAOIFI SS45 places capital-protection reserves in investors' profit, not in the manager's share, and a manager may not guarantee investors' capital in a mudarabah. Whether Trade's manager-funded stock and a backstop from Sacred's raised capital are acceptable needs review, and may change their structure.

### Borrow

1. The Arc and USDC premise above.
2. The exact sale from owned inventory, repayable in USDC.
3. The binding rebate formula.
4. The restricted mudarabah, investor-owned reserve, loss carry and the pilot backstop.
5. Collateral enforcement, and any keeper or service compensation.
6. Eligibility of each collateral asset.

Rulings are not independent, so a no on one may change others. Each board candidate is asked about these before engagement.

## What the design does on purpose

- **No sale back to the vault.** Pledged assets are sold on the open market. Buying back what you sold on credit is prohibited.
- **No organized tawarruq.** Trade buys a real asset the trader wants. Borrow delivers USDC directly, with no simulated commodity movement. [IIFA Resolution 179](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/33707.html) distinguishes individual from organized and inverse tawarruq.
- **No circular refinancing.** A new sale needs real settlement and independent funding ([IIFA](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/49710.html)).
- **Careful wording.** Sacred says you own the asset and it is pledged, and it describes backstops by the order they absorb a loss. It never says guaranteed.

## Precedent and limits

- No fatwa covers leverage on crypto, approving or rejecting.
- Some scholars reject crypto itself. A June 2026 fatwa from Darul Uloom Karachi holds that crypto tokens are not property. A board following it would reject the whole design.
- If a board says no, the design changes.

## Not in this design

| Left out | Why | What would bring it back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shorts | The only classical route, a prepaid forward (*salam*), needs its own market and rulings | A later phase |
| Rollover | Extending a debt for an extra charge is riba | Never |
| Gold tokens | Gold must be exchanged on the spot | Never |
| Synthetic perps | Nothing is owned, and settlement is in cash (Res. 40–41, 157, 238) | Not expected |
| Hedging the vault | Every available hedge is a short or a derivative | Nothing currently |
