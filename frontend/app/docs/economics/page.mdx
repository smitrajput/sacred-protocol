# Economics

What traders pay, what depositors earn, what the protocol keeps, and why the built version will be re-cut before scale. Every figure here is a simulation on 2018 to 2026 prices under stated demand assumptions, not a forecast.

## The money, in version one

| Flow | Size | Goes to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Markup** | 10% a year on the financed amount, plus 1% above 2x | 100% to depositors; 10% of it builds the bucket's loss reserve until 5% |
| **Profit share** | 30% of a winning ticket's profit | The backstop fund: 20% treasury, 15% liquidity reserve, 65% streamed to stakers over 90 days |
| **Pool fee** | 1% on SCR buys, 3% on sells, in USDC | 20% treasury; the rest to the reserve until its target, then to the fund |
| **Keeper repayment** | 1 USDC a call | Whoever settles or runs the cut-off |

Nothing else is charged. The manager takes no share of the markup. A bucket 80% financed at 11% pays depositors about 7.9% a year before shortfalls: 100,000 USDC earns 168.77 in a week, 16.88 goes to the reserve, 151.89 raises the share price.

## What the simulation found

A mechanism-design review treated the founder as the rule-setter and each party as a player with one profitable deviation to close, then simulated the whole system: real tickets on Binance one-minute prices from January 2018 to September 2026, a daily model of deposits, rates, reserves and fund on top, and 1,000 bootstrapped five-year paths with the real crash clustering. Leverage demand follows perp funding, so bear demand is about 0.4 of bull demand.

At its own equilibrium the built version pays depositors about 5%, charges traders about five times the market price of leverage, and cannot offer 5x. The nine findings, largest first:

| # | Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The 30% profit share is a tax on volatility, not on profit.** Charged per ticket with no netting of losses | A trader rolling 14-day BTC 3x tickets shares on 135% of exposure a year. All-in cost is 48% of exposure on BTC 3x, 61% on ETH 2x. Perp funding averaged 11.7%, and 3.3% in bear markets. A 3x ticket beats holding only if BTC rises faster than 72% a year |
| 2 | **About a tenth of what traders pay reaches depositors** | Markup is 1.7 of every 16.6 paid. The yield ceiling is 85% × 10.5% × 0.9 = 8.0%; simulated average 5.3%, 3.9% in bear markets |
| 3 | **Nothing ties deposits to demand** | Free entry pushes yield down to depositors' outside option; utilisation falls to about 40% in bear markets |
| 4 | **5x cannot run in term mode** | Shortfalls cost 9.6% of the financed amount a year on BTC 5x; depositors lose in 69% of years |
| 5 | **The protocol earns 6% of the profit share and nothing from markup** | Income is lumpy and follows trader profit, so the manager is paid for volume, not depositor safety |
| 6 | **Staked SCR adds little cover** | A shortfall sale capped 10% below average price raises about 5% of the pool's USDC per event |
| 7 | **Exits are priced a little too high in a crash** | A ticket at 102% cover with a week left carries an unmarked expected shortfall near 2.5% |
| 8 | **A trader can move profit outside the share** | Sandwiching one's own open and close inside the 1% tolerance shifts up to 2% of exposure per ticket |
| 9 | **The liquidity reserve refills only in good times** | A 30-minute average is cheap to depress; a 10% discount ceiling lets a holder cycle SCR through the sale for 6.7% a round |

## The proposed mechanism

Eighteen changes in five groups. Each keeps the sale, pledge and partnership; none adds a charge to a ticket after it opens. The simulation's own summary is that two of the founder's three outcomes can be made to hold in bull and bear markets, and the third is a scale question no mechanism delivers.

| Group | Today | Proposed | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Price to traders** | 10% markup plus 1% above 2x; 30% share per ticket | Markup 15.5% up to 85% utilisation, rising to 30% at the 92% cap, plus a gap premium. Profit share 10% of *net* profit, losses carried forward 90 days. Profit measured at feed prices | The share base falls from 135% to 90% of exposure. All-in cost falls by about 60%, to inside what dYdX, Hyperliquid and GMX collect per dollar of open interest |
| **Split** | All markup to depositors | 85% depositors, 10% manager, 5% loss reserve. A 9% target payout: profit above it fills a payout reserve up to 6% of deposits, months below draw on it | Protocol income that is paid in bear markets. Smoothing is accepted practice (IFSB GN-3) and must be disclosed |
| **Capacity** | A static bucket cap | Admit deposits only while utilisation is above 85%, at most 5% a week; return idle USDC pro rata if utilisation stays under 70% for 28 days | The rule that holds 9% in bear markets. Without it the median month pays 7.0% |
| **Leverage** | BTC 3x, ETH 2x, term only | Term unchanged. Margin mode 3x to 5x with part of the coin sold at 110% cover, restored to 115%. No limit on ticket count; limits on size. Keeper fee indexed to gas, two independent keepers | 5x costs 0.12% a year on BTC with a working keeper, 2.3% with an hour's outage on every sale |
| **Backstop and reserve** | Staked SCR counted as a loss layer | Staked SCR kept as the last layer, sized at zero in solvency tests; the fund holds USDC to 8% of the financed amount before paying stakers. Reserve-sale discount capped at the sell tax plus one point; price off a 24-hour average floored at 90% of the 7-day low; reserve seeded from the raise | Closes findings 6 and 9. An 8% buffer cuts loss years under outage stress from 6% to 1.5% |

Under the proposal every party's best move is to stay and play straight, in both regimes. The profit share is the one dial to tune with launch data: start at 10%, raise toward 20% only if measured demand barely responds to price.

## What it pays, per 100 of demand

| | Bull | Chop | Bear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deposits | 70 | 70 | 48 |
| Utilisation | 83% | 77% | 71% |
| Markup rate | 21% | 20% | 17% |
| Depositor payout a year | 9.1% | 9.2% | 9.4% |
| Total fees a year | 19.7 | 13.1 | 7.9 |
| Kept by the protocol | 4.3 | 2.5 | 1.6 |

Fees run at about 23% of deposits a year across the cycle and the protocol keeps 4.8%. The system does not hold fees flat: it shrinks in a bear market and keeps its ratios. The 9% is earned on money in the vault; about 30% of deposits are handed back in a bear market.

## Scale

| Target | Deposits needed, cycle average | To hold it through a bear market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500k a month in total fees | 26m, carrying about 27m of exposure | 47m on average, 36m in the bear |
| 500k a month kept by the protocol | 124m | 237m on average |

Comparable protocols trade at 2 to 24 times yearly fees and 6 to 30 times yearly revenue: GMX 6x, Maple 20x, Aave 30x. On those multiples a 6 million valuation needs 200k to 1 million of yearly protocol revenue, which the proposal earns on 4 to 21 million of deposits.

## Treasury, in version one

One treasury pays for development. In version one its income is 20% of the backstop fund's receipts, which in a rising market is roughly 1,500 a month at 1 million of deposits, 7,500 at 5 million, 15,000 at 10 million, assuming winning tickets earn 25% of exposure a year, which is a guess. It is near zero in a falling market. Fees do not cover a founder's costs until deposits reach roughly 5 million; the treasury's token stream carries the early years, and before launch the build, the audit and the board need outside money. This is the case for the manager's 10% of markup in the proposal: income that is paid in every regime.

## What the simulation does not fix

- **Demand is unproven.** Every fee figure scales with halal leverage demand, and proven on-chain halal demand is in single-digit millions. Treat deposits above 10 million as a distribution assumption.
- **The price cut is a bet on elasticity.** Charging 60% less needs about 2.7 times the volume to match today's fees. Measure the response during the guarded launch before fixing the share.
- **5x rests on two rulings**: forced sale before the due date, and a 5x ceiling, which the design gives 35 to 45% odds. If refused, the term-only version still pays 9% but stops at BTC 3x and ETH 2x.
- **Keeper or sequencer outage is the tail.** An hour's outage on every forced sale: depositors lose in 1.5% of years, worst year 7.1%.
- **Bear-market traders pay about five times the conventional price**, 17% against 3.3% funding. The only verified Islamic margin rate found is about 11% a year, at 2x on equities.
- **Launch starts with empty buffers.** Seed the 8% fund buffer from the raise, or cap the first tickets until it fills.

Not modelled: the liquidity reserve and reserve sales, on-chain slippage at size, contract or oracle failure, a USDC or wrapped-BTC event, a crash worse than any since 2018, SCR's price, and traders who do anything other than hold to the due date.
