# Thesis

Two billion people are told that among the two biggest things in DeFi, yield and leverage, are forbidden to them. Sacred is built on the observation that the prohibition is on the *structure* and its *risks*, not the *outcome*, and that a different structure with better risk tradeoffs gives similar or better outcomes with fewer downsides.

## The market that is locked out

- **Leverage.** Perps and margin run on interest, funding payments and selling what you do not own. In 2006 the Muslim World League's Fiqh Council ruled against margin trading on four grounds: fees for delay, a loan tied to a sale, selling what is not owned, and excessive risk.
- **Yield.** Majority stablecoin yield in DeFi comes from lending at interest. That is textbook-definition riba.

So a Muslim trader sits out or trades against their own faith, and a Muslim saver holds idle stablecoins. Both are large groups. Both have no product. The whole of Islamic DeFi held under 50 million dollars in mid-2026; the largest halal protocol tracked by DefiLlama holds 4 million. The demand is unproven on chain, and that is the bet: a market this size with this many people in it has never been offered a product that is conducive to their values.

## The novel primitive

Custodied selling, over lending.

There are 2 sides to this mechanism. Stablecoin depositors on one side i.e. supply side, and leverage traders on the other i.e. demand side.

Stablecoin deposits on the supply side are done in a profit-sharing vault which has a mudarabah structure (an islamic finance structure with a capital supplier and a manager, where they share the profits when the manager makes money, but in case of losses only the supplier bears it). 

And on the demand side, the trader deposits a down payment for a commodity-like asset they want leveraged exposure to, then the desk (technically a vault too) buys that asset from the market and sells it to the trader at cost plus a fixed markup, paid later, but keeps its custody (hence a desk) until the balance is paid. This is murabaha, the cost-plus sale Islamic banks already use to finance a home or a car, applied to a crypto spot market. The desk theoretically buys the asset, sells it to the trader and takes it back as a pledge (rahn) in one transaction, as a security for the unpaid amount owed by the trader to the desk (onchain this will only be the vault making a swap from USDC to ETH for instance). Mudarabah, murabaha and rahn are standard Islamic finance contracts in practice by major Islamic banks since decades.

What makes it a *better* product and not merely a permitted one:

| A perp | A ticket |
| --- | --- |
| Funding paid every hour, set by how crowded one side is. The total cost is unknown until you close | Markup fixed at opening; the whole cost is known before you commit. No counterparty is needed, so a crowded market costs nothing extra |
| Liquidation price. At 10x, a 9% wick closes the position in full, even if the price recovers an hour later | Term mode has no liquidation price. Price drops do not close a ticket before its due date |
| A contract against another trader; no asset is owned. In cross margin, one position's loss can take the whole account | The trader owns the actual asset, bought on the spot market and pledged. Each ticket stands alone; the most it can lose is its down payment |
| Perp vaults (HLP, GLP, JLP) earn by trading against traders: their losses, spreads and liquidations | Depositors earn the markup whichever way the price moves. They lose only if the asset sells for less than what the trader still owes, and that gap is bigger than the loss reserve, backstop fund and staked SCR together |

The desk is the one piece that is new. Nothing like it exists on chain. Everything else in the plan composes around it.

## Why the economics can work

A mechanism-design simulation over Binance prices from 2018 to 2026, with 1,000 bootstrapped five-year paths, found that the built version pays depositors about 5% and charges traders about five times the market price of leverage, and that a redesign of the splits and a capacity rule takes depositors to a 9% target payout in 99.7% of simulated months, bull and bear, with traders paying 17 to 25% of exposure a year: inside what perp venues collect per dollar of open interest. Total fees run at about 23% of deposits a year; the protocol keeps 4.8%. The whole analysis, with what it does not fix, is on the [economics page](/docs/economics).

The revenue question is then a demand question. 500k a month in total fees needs about 26 million of deposits on a cycle average. On the multiples comparable protocols trade at (2 to 24 times fees, 6 to 30 times revenue), a 6 million valuation needs 200k to 1 million of yearly protocol revenue, which the proposal earns on 4 to 21 million of deposits.

## Why now

- **Stablecoins and tokenised assets are on chain at scale**, so the thing being sold is real, liquid and provable, which is exactly what murabaha requires.
- **A 2026 Islamic DeFi field with under 50 million in it** and no leverage product at all.
- **The code is done.** Term mode is deployed on Arbitrum One with 279 Foundry tests, invariants, a fork test and browser flows. The open work is the board, the audit and the cohort, not the build.

## What this is not

Not certified: no Shariah board has reviewed the design, and the design's own estimate for the term-mode package at 3x is 40 to 50% odds of approval, with fallbacks for each ruling. Not audited. Not a promise of any yield: a mudarabah cannot promise a return, only target a payout and smooth it. Every number on these pages is a proposal, a backtest or a simulation, and the [risk page](/docs/risk) says what the simulations cannot capture.
