# Economics

What traders pay, what depositors earn, what the protocol keeps, and the simulation the dials were cut from. Every figure here is a simulation on 2018 to 2026 prices under stated demand assumptions, not a forecast.

## The money

| Flow | Size | Goes to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Markup** | 15.5% a year on the financed amount while the bucket is at or under 85% financed, rising in a straight line to 30% at the 92% cap. Plus a gap premium: 1.5% on 3x term positions, 0.5% on margin positions | 85% to depositors, 10% to the manager, 5% to the bucket's loss reserve until it holds 3% of the bucket |
| **Payout** | A 9% a year target. Markup above it fills a payout reserve up to 6% of deposits; months below it draw on the reserve | Once the reserve is full, the surplus is split equally between depositors and the manager |
| **Profit share** | 10% of a trader's net profit, losses carried forward 90 days, measured at feed prices | 30% treasury, 10% to the liquidity reserve until it reaches its target, the rest to the backstop fund. The fund keeps USDC to 8% of the financed amount and streams only the excess to stakers over 90 days |
| **Pool fee** | 1% on SCR buys, 3% on sells, in USDC | 20% treasury; the rest to the reserve until its target, then to the fund |
| **Keeper repayment** | A fee indexed to gas at the time of the call, capped at 20 USDC | Whoever settles, sells or runs the cut-off |

Nothing else is charged. A BTC bucket 85% financed at 17% earns 12.3% a year for depositors before shortfalls: 100,000 USDC earns 277.12 in a week, 27.71 goes to the manager, 13.86 to the loss reserve, 235.55 to depositors. The 9% target is paid, and the rest fills the payout reserve.

Deposits follow demand. New deposits are admitted only while utilisation is above 85%, at most 5% growth a week, and idle USDC is returned pro rata if utilisation stays under 70% for 28 days. Without that rule the median month pays 7.0%.

## What the simulation found

A mechanism-design review treated the founder as the rule-setter and each party as a player with one profitable deviation to close, then simulated the whole system: real positions on Binance one-minute prices from January 2018 to September 2026, a daily model of deposits, rates, reserves and fund on top, and 1,000 bootstrapped six-year paths with the real crash clustering. Leverage demand follows perp funding, so bear demand is about 0.4 of bull demand.

The first design charged 10% a year plus 1% above 2x, took 30% of each winning position's profit, and paid all the markup to depositors. At its own equilibrium it paid depositors about 5%, charged traders about five times the market price of leverage, and could not offer 5x. The nine findings, largest first, are what the dials above answer:

| # | Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A 30% profit share per position is a tax on volatility, not on profit.** Charged per position with no netting of losses | A trader rolling 14-day BTC 3x positions shares on 135% of exposure a year. All-in cost was 48% of exposure on BTC 3x, 61% on ETH 2x. Perp funding averaged 11.7%, and 3.3% in bear markets. A 3x position beat holding only if BTC rose faster than 72% a year |
| 2 | **About a tenth of what traders paid reached depositors** | Markup was 1.7 of every 16.6 paid. The yield ceiling was 85% × 10.5% × 0.9 = 8.0%; simulated average 5.3%, 3.9% in bear markets |
| 3 | **Nothing tied deposits to demand** | Free entry pushes yield down to depositors' outside option; utilisation falls to about 40% in bear markets |
| 4 | **5x cannot run in term mode** | Shortfalls cost 9.6% of the financed amount a year on BTC 5x; depositors lose in 69% of years |
| 5 | **The protocol earned 6% of the profit share and nothing from markup** | Income is lumpy and follows trader profit, so the manager is paid for volume, not depositor safety |
| 6 | **Staked SCR adds little cover** | A shortfall sale capped 10% below average price raises about 5% of the pool's USDC per event |
| 7 | **Exits were priced a little too high in a crash** | A position at 102% cover with a week left carries an unmarked expected shortfall near 2.5% |
| 8 | **A trader could move profit outside the share** | Sandwiching one's own open and close inside a 1% tolerance shifts up to 2% of exposure per position |
| 9 | **The liquidity reserve refills only in good times** | A 30-minute average is cheap to depress; a 10% discount ceiling lets a holder cycle SCR through the sale for 6.7% a round |

## What changed, and why

Eighteen changes in five groups. Each keeps the sale, pledge and partnership; none adds a charge to a position after it opens.

| Group | First design | At launch | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Price to traders** | 10% markup plus 1% above 2x; 30% share per position | Markup 15.5% up to 85% utilisation, rising to 30% at the 92% cap, plus a gap premium. Profit share 10% of *net* profit, losses carried forward 90 days. Profit measured at feed prices | The share base falls from 135% to 90% of exposure. All-in cost falls by about 60%, to inside what dYdX, Hyperliquid and GMX collect per dollar of open interest |
| **Split** | All markup to depositors | 85% depositors, 10% manager, 5% loss reserve. A 9% target payout: profit above it fills a payout reserve up to 6% of deposits, months below draw on it | Protocol income that is paid in bear markets. Smoothing is accepted practice (IFSB GN-3) and must be disclosed |
| **Capacity** | A static bucket cap | Admit deposits only while utilisation is above 85%, at most 5% a week; return idle USDC pro rata if utilisation stays under 70% for 28 days | The rule that holds 9% in bear markets. Without it the median month pays 7.0% |
| **Leverage** | BTC 3x, ETH 2x, term only | Term unchanged. Margin mode 3x to 5x with part of the asset sold at 110% cover, restored to 115%. No limit on position count; limits on size. Keeper fee indexed to gas, two independent keepers | 5x costs 0.12% a year on BTC with a working keeper, 2.3% with an hour's outage on every sale |
| **Backstop and reserve** | Staked SCR counted as a loss layer | Staked SCR kept as the last layer, sized at zero in solvency tests; the fund holds USDC to 8% of the financed amount before paying stakers. Reserve-sale discount capped at the sell tax plus one point; price off a 24-hour average floored at 90% of the 7-day low; reserve seeded from the raise | Closes findings 6 and 9. An 8% buffer cuts loss years under outage stress from 6% to 1.5% |

Under these dials every party's best move is to stay and play straight, in both regimes. The profit share is the one dial to tune with launch data: start at 10%, raise toward 20% only if measured demand barely responds to price.

## What it pays, per 100 of demand

| | Bull | Chop | Bear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deposits | 70 | 70 | 48 |
| Utilisation | 83% | 77% | 71% |
| Markup rate | 21% | 20% | 17% |
| Depositor payout a year | 9.1% | 9.2% | 9.4% |
| Total fees a year | 19.7 | 13.1 | 7.9 |
| Kept by the protocol | 4.3 | 2.5 | 1.6 |

Fees run at about 23% of deposits a year across the cycle and the protocol keeps 4.8%. The system does not hold fees flat: it shrinks in a bear market and keeps its ratios. The 9% is earned on money in the vault; about 30% of deposits are handed back in a bear market. The payout held at 9% or more in 99.6% of simulated months, and reached 11% or more in one month in ten, when the reserve was full and the surplus was shared.

## Scale

| Target | Deposits needed, cycle average | To hold it through a bear market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500k a month in total fees | 26m, carrying about 27m of exposure | 47m on average, 36m in the bear |
| 500k a month kept by the protocol | 124m | 237m on average |

Comparable protocols trade at 2 to 24 times yearly fees and 6 to 30 times yearly revenue: GMX 6x, Maple 20x, Aave 30x. On those multiples a 6 million valuation needs 200k to 1 million of yearly protocol revenue, which the mechanism earns on 4 to 21 million of deposits.

## Treasury

One treasury pays for development. Its income is the manager's 10% of markup, paid in every regime, plus 30% of the profit share and 20% of pool fees, which arrive mostly in rising markets. In the simulation the protocol keeps about 4.8% of deposits a year across the cycle: roughly 4,000 a month at 1 million of deposits, 20,000 at 5 million, 40,000 at 10 million. Fees do not cover a founder's costs until deposits reach several million; the treasury's token stream carries the early years, and before launch the build, the audit and the board need outside money.

## What the simulation does not fix

- **Demand is unproven.** Every fee figure scales with halal leverage demand, and proven on-chain halal demand is in single-digit millions. Treat deposits above 10 million as a distribution assumption.
- **The price cut is a bet on elasticity.** Charging 60% less than the first design needs about 2.7 times its volume to match its fees. Measure the response during the guarded launch before fixing the share.
- **5x rests on two rulings**: forced sale before the due date, and a 5x ceiling, which the design gives 35 to 45% odds. If refused, the term-only version still pays 9% but stops at BTC 3x and ETH 2x.
- **Keeper or sequencer outage is the tail.** An hour's outage on every forced sale: depositors lose in 1.5% of years, worst year 7.1%.
- **Bear-market traders pay about five times the conventional price**, 17% against 3.3% funding. The only verified Islamic margin rate found is about 11% a year, at 2x on equities.
- **Launch starts with empty buffers.** Seed the 8% fund buffer and the liquidity reserve from the raise, or cap the first positions until they fill.
- **The launch dials are not yet in the deployed code.** The contracts on Arbitrum One run the first design's dials; the [status page](/docs/status) lists what changes before launch.

Not modelled: the liquidity reserve and reserve sales, on-chain slippage at size, contract or oracle failure, a USDC or wrapped-BTC event, a crash worse than any since 2018, SCR's price, and traders who do anything other than hold to the due date.
