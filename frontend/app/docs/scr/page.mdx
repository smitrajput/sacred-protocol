# SCR: holders, stakers, reserve buyers

SCR is the protocol token. Staked SCR stands behind shortfalls, sales of SCR fund a USDC liquidity reserve, and one SCR/USDC pool gives it a market. Depositors' yield does not depend on it.

| Part | Holds | Money in | Money out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Backstop fund** | Staked SCR and USDC | 60% of the traders' 10% net-profit share | Shortfalls, then a stream to stakers from whatever exceeds 8% of the financed amount |
| **Liquidity reserve** | USDC | Reserve sales, pool fees, 10% of the profit share until the reserve is at its target, seed money from the raise | Deposits into the vault when it is short of cash |
| **Pool** | SCR and USDC, owned by the protocol | Swap fees | Fees to the reserve, then to the fund |

## If you stake

- Anyone can stake. Leaving takes a **14-day cooldown followed by a 7-day window**, as long as the longest term position, so nobody can run ahead of a loss. Shares in cooldown still earn and can still be sold for a shortfall. Staked SCR cannot be transferred.
- You are paid **in USDC** from the traders' profit share, streamed over 90 days, and only from what the fund holds above 8% of the amount financed. There are no SCR emissions and no rebase. The fund's income is trade profit only; in a falling market it receives little or nothing, and after a loss it pays nothing until the buffer is refilled.
- When a desk's loss reserve cannot cover a shortfall, the fund pays from its USDC first. If that is not enough, staked SCR is sold in the pool, **up to 30% of the stake per event, stopping 10% below the pool's average price**. Whatever is still uncovered falls on the vault's depositors.
- One fund covers every desk, and every desk draws on the one vault. The protocol stakes some of its own SCR.

The simulation's view: a shortfall sale at that cap raises only about 5% of the pool's USDC per event, so the real backstop is the fund's USDC. That is why staked SCR is the last layer, sized at zero in solvency tests, and why stakers are paid the fund's excess only: about 8% of deposits a year in simulation. A staker's reward and whether staked SCR may be sold for a shortfall are rulings for the board; the founder's position is that stakers are loss-bearing partners, not paid guarantors.

## If you buy in the reserve sale

The reserve sells new SCR for USDC and delivers it **staked**, so the cooldown applies. This is how the liquidity reserve that backs withdrawals is built.

- **Price.** The pool's 24-hour average price less a 4% discount, floored at 90% of the pool's 7-day low. The discount can never exceed the pool's sell tax plus one point.
- **Open only while the reserve is below its target**, and capped per period at 0.05% of supply a week, so the 10% set aside lasts about four years.
- **Sales stop by themselves** in a bear market, because nobody buys, so the reserve is seeded from the raise and topped up by a tenth of the profit share until it reaches its target.
- **Pool fee on your way out**: 1% on buys, 3% on sells, in USDC, with a 5% ceiling fixed in code. The fund's own shortfall sales are exempt.

Two attacks the review found, and what closes them: a 30-minute average could be depressed for the cost of the sell tax, which is why the price is a 24-hour average with a floor; and a discount above the sell tax would let a holder sell liquid SCR and buy it back staked for a gain each cycle (6.7% at a 10% discount), which is why the ceiling is the sell tax plus one point. Idle reserve cash never earns interest; it may go into tokenised sukuk later. It is not called a bond.

## If you hold

The pool is one SCR/USDC pool on Uniswap v4, owned by the protocol: 4% of supply plus USDC from the raise, never withdrawn. Anyone can open another pool without the fee, so this income leaks and the design does not depend on it. Of a trader's net profit, 90% stays with the trader and 10% is shared; of that, 30% goes to the treasury, 10% to the reserve until it reaches its target, and the rest to the fund, which pays stakers only what it holds above 8% of the financed amount. Pool fees go 20% to the treasury, then to the reserve until its target, then to the fund. Markup is not in this picture: 85% of it goes to depositors, 10% to the manager and 5% to the loss reserve of the desk that earned it.

## Points before the token

Traders earn one point per USDC of markup paid. Depositors earn one point per USDC per day deposited, counted at each weekly cut-off. Volume earns nothing, so farming points means paying depositors. Points are a discretionary gift, not a promised return; they are computed from on-chain events by the open indexer, so anyone can check their own. Airdrops arrive as staked SCR, which builds the backstop from day one.

## Constraints any token here inherits

No guaranteed return to anyone, in any form. No fee for a guarantee: a backstop must be donated, mutual, or true loss-sharing capital. No senior and junior claims inside one pool. No charge can be added to a position. Rewards must be discretionary gifts or a real share of profit, never a stipulated return on a debt. The token itself needs screening.
